# Requisition

## Role summary

Platform Engineer, Platform Engineering team, Technology.

The role builds and maintains the container platform and deployment pipelines that Contoso's product teams deploy onto. It is the third engineer on a team of two that currently supports eleven product teams, and its focus is the self-service half of the platform: making the paths product teams take most often safe, documented and unattended.

## Team and reporting line

Platform Engineering, within Technology. Reports to Tomas Berg, Platform Engineering Lead, who is the hiring manager for this requisition.

The team is Tomas plus two engineers. It sits alongside Cloud Operations and Information Security, and has a standing dependency on both for anything that changes the deployment path.

## Key responsibilities

- Build and maintain the container platform and its deployment pipelines
- Own the self-service developer paths: templates, scaffolding, documentation
- Carry the platform on-call rotation, one week in three
- Work with Information Security to keep the deployment path's controls current
- Reduce the platform work product teams currently raise tickets for

## Justification

Platform Engineering has supported a growing number of product teams on a fixed headcount of two for eighteen months. The team's ticket queue has grown from a weekly average of nine to twenty-six over that period, and the median time to close has gone from two days to nine. The work that has been displaced is the self-service and documentation work that would reduce the queue — so the queue grows because the team is too busy serving it.

The on-call rotation is also currently one week in two across two engineers, which is not sustainable and was raised as a retention risk in the last two quarterly reviews.

If the role is not filled, the practical consequence is that the platform stays ticket-driven: product teams keep raising requests for work the platform should do unattended, and the two engineers stay on a one-in-two rotation. The team has said it can hold this position, not that it can improve from it.

## Engagement type

Fixed term

## Term or expected duration

— not stated —

## Engagement rationale

The work is ongoing and the on-call rotation requires it. A contractor can build a deployment path but cannot be the third name on a permanent rotation, and the retention risk this role is partly meant to address is not helped by a temporary appointment.

Platform Engineering has used contractors twice before for platform work — a migration in 2024 and a pipeline rebuild in 2025 — and both left capability the remaining team then had to re-learn. That experience is the reason this was not proposed as a fixed-term engagement.

## Role evaluation method

Evaluated 9 February 2026 by Tomas Berg (Platform Engineering Lead) with Hana Te Rangi (HR Business Partner), against the two existing Platform Engineer roles in the team and against the Senior Platform Engineer role advertised by Contoso in 2025.

The method, written down here because it is not documented anywhere else: the draft responsibilities were compared line by line against the two incumbent roles' descriptions; the differences were the self-service and documentation ownership, which the incumbents share informally rather than hold. Those differences were judged not to change the band. The on-call obligation was confirmed as identical to the incumbents'. HR confirmed the band against the remuneration framework and no benchmarking data was purchased.

This took about ninety minutes and produced no document other than this record.

## Role evaluation outcome

Evaluated at Technology Band 4, the same band as the two existing Platform Engineer roles. Remuneration range confirmed by HR against the current framework.

The role as evaluated is unchanged from the role as described in the requisition.

## Role evaluation notes

The self-service and documentation ownership was the only point of discussion. It is genuinely a difference from the incumbent roles, and the judgement that it does not change the band rests on it being a focus rather than an additional accountability. If the team later formalises that ownership as a distinct accountability, the two incumbent roles should be re-evaluated rather than this one.

All four met the first three. The fourth criterion separated them: two wrote clearly about work they had done and two described work in terms that did not survive a follow-up question.

## Process gaps

**Nothing detects an unseen payroll request.** The single largest delay on this hire was five days lost because an automated email went to a spam folder and no one knew. The process budgets twenty-four hours for a payroll rework; this took five days, and it was caught by someone thinking to telephone rather than by anything in the process. It is the third occurrence in six months. A start date survived this time only because the candidate had already moved it out a week for her own reasons.

**The non-standard hardware decision has no defined path.** The process asks the question and stops. On this hire the hiring manager decided on precedent and told the service desk, which worked because the manager was experienced and the precedent existed. Neither is guaranteed. The two days the decision cost were not budgeted in any estimate.

**Building access is outside the provisioning sequence and unaware of it.** Cards are produced in a weekly batch that the provisioning run knows nothing about. Any Monday start will arrive without a card unless someone remembers to request it early.

**One system still provisions by hand.** The observability platform has no directory integration and is created manually for every starter. It has not failed yet; nothing would catch it if it did.

**The role evaluation and advertising steps remain undocumented.** Both were carried out properly on this hire and both are recorded here only because this definition asks for them. There is still no written definition of either, and the record in `role-evaluation.method` is currently the closest thing to one that exists.
